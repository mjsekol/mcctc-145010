# Phase Guide · Control
## 145010 Senior Capstone · Weeks 15-18

**Why this phase exists.** A project you understand and a project someone else can use are two
different things. In the next four weeks you find out which one you have, fix what you can, and
hand the project to a person who has to live with it after you graduate.

**Why it is four weeks and not one.** Most students plan to finish building in Week 16 and "wrap
up" in Weeks 17-18. That plan fails, because testing with real people always finds something,
acceptance always finds something, and handoff always takes longer than it looks. **These weeks
are not slack. They are where the Stakeholder Outcome score is earned.**

**What you are producing.** A usability study with five real users, corrections with re-tests, a
signed acceptance record, a rollout plan, a user guide and training materials, a completed handoff,
a thirty-day survival record, a repeated baseline measurement, and your final presentation.

**Competencies this phase evidences:** 2.12.3, 2.12.4, 2.12.5, 2.12.6 (tests and acceptance),
2.13.1 to 2.13.6 (rollout and handoff), 6.5.10 (usability tests on a completed site), 1.10.2
(customer needs), 1.10.3 (communicate features, benefits, and warranties), 1.10.4 (improvement
procedures), 1.10.5 (satisfaction measured with tools), 1.5.5 (bias in who a product serves),
6.1.4 (drafting, revising, editing, and proofreading), 1.2.2 (formal presentation), 1.2.12
(technical writing and reports), 1.1.2, 1.1.4, 1.1.5 (careers, professional organizations, and
the portfolio).

## Week 15 · usability testing

**The goal:** five real people, outside this class, using what you built while you stay quiet.

You ran a usability study in 145130. This one is on a finished product for a real stakeholder,
and the rules are the same.

### The rules for working with real people

**These are conditions, not advice. Breaking one stops the session.**

1. **They say yes out loud** after you read the consent script as written.
2. **No recording of any kind.** No video, no audio, no screen capture, no photographs. You write
   on paper.
3. **No personal data leaves the session.** Participants are P1 to P5. No names, no job titles, no
   ages, nothing that identifies them.
4. **No classmates in the formal five.** A classmate is for the pilot.
5. **Nothing from a session goes into an AI tool.** Not the notes, not a summary.
6. **Never alone with an adult you do not know.** Sessions happen at school, on an approved call,
   or during a visit the school arranged.
7. **You thank every participant in writing** before the last day of the course.

### Who the five are

**The best participants are the people who will actually use the project**, reached through your
stakeholder and your instructor. When that is not possible: adults in this building outside the
program, students in other programs, and family members at home, with the same rules. Your
instructor keeps a list of adults who have agreed.

**Think about who is missing.** If all five are the same kind of person, your findings describe
that kind of person. Write down who your five do not represent. That is your 1.5.5 evidence.

### The week

| Day | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Monday** | Finish the [Usability Test Protocol](MCCTC_145010_Template_UsabilityTestProtocol.md). Pilot it with a classmate. Fix the tasks the pilot breaks. Confirm five sessions with times. |
| **Tuesday to Thursday** | Run sessions. One [Observation Sheet](MCCTC_145010_Template_UsabilityObservationSheet.md) per participant. Write up each session the same day. Period 8 is for sessions too. |
| **Friday** | Findings and the prioritized change list, committed. Weekly update with what you found. Corrections start. |

**The trap, and it is the one everybody falls into.** A participant struggles and you start
explaining why the design is right. The moment you defend the design, you have stopped learning
from the session. **What they did is the data. Your reasons are not.** Write it down and say
nothing.

## Week 16 · corrections and acceptance

**The goal:** the stakeholder decides, in writing, against the criteria you both signed in Week 8.

**The WebXam 145010 post-test is this week.** Your instructor sets the day. It triggers your
college credit through the survey at the end. Do the Strand 6 review reps before it.

**Milestone Review 4 is this week.**

| Day | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Monday and Tuesday** | Corrections from the change list, highest priority first. Every correction gets a re-test. Re-run your whole test plan after the last correction, not only the fixed cases. |
| **Wednesday and Thursday** | **The acceptance run with the stakeholder.** In person with your instructor present, or on a call your instructor approved. |
| **Friday** | [Acceptance Record](MCCTC_145010_Template_AcceptanceRecord.md) complete and signed. The signed copy goes to your instructor. Weekly update. |

### The acceptance run, in five rules

1. **You run the procedure the stakeholder agreed**, the committed version. Not a newer one.
2. **You do not change a case because it is about to fail.** That is the whole reason the
   procedure exists.
3. **A surprise failure is written down, not fixed in front of them.** "That is a real failure. I
   will fix it and re-run it."
4. **If the stakeholder does not understand a case, that is a finding** about your writing.
5. **Something not accepted is recorded honestly** with what you agreed to do about it. A record
   with a known issue in it is an honest record, not a weak one.

**If the stakeholder cannot attend,** send the procedure and your results in writing and ask for
their written decision. Record it as decided in writing. **Never run it alone and record that they
were there.**

## Week 17 · rollout and handoff

**The goal:** the stakeholder can run, use, and get help with the project without you.

| Day | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Monday** | [Rollout Plan](MCCTC_145010_Template_RolloutPlan.md): goals, timeline, support staff, training needs, contingency plans. |
| **Tuesday** | Rollout plan **sent to the stakeholder**, with a request for any changes. This is 2.13.2, and "in a timely manner" means before the handoff, not at it. |
| **Wednesday** | [User Guide](MCCTC_145010_Template_UserGuide.md) and training materials finished and proofread. Repeat your baseline measurement, the same way as Week 9. |
| **Thursday** | Training with the stakeholder, or the stakeholder works through the user guide and confirms in writing. Finish the baseline comparison. |
| **Friday** | [Handoff Checklist](MCCTC_145010_Template_HandoffChecklist.md) complete, including the final test on the delivered system and the **final review and approval**. [Thirty-Day Survival Record](MCCTC_145010_Template_ThirtyDaySurvival.md) complete. Weekly update. [Presentation outline](MCCTC_145010_Template_FinalPresentationOutline.md) drafted. |

**Final review and approval "according to company standards"** means you ask your stakeholder
what their organization needs before it accepts something. Sometimes it is their word. Sometimes
their manager has to agree. Ask in Week 16, not on Friday of Week 17.

**Your warranty.** Your user guide and handoff say, in plain words, what you will fix and until
when. For most capstones the honest answer is: until the end of Week 18, and after that only what
you and the stakeholder agree in writing through the school. Promising support you cannot give is
worse than promising none.

## If your stakeholder disappears

It happens. People change jobs, get sick, and get busy. **You are not scored down for a
stakeholder's silence that your contact log documents.** You are scored down for silence of your
own.

1. **Two contacts with no reply over five school days:** send the follow-up in the
   [Stakeholder Communication Guide](MCCTC_145010_Guide_StakeholderCommunication.md#8-when-your-stakeholder-goes-quiet),
   and tell your instructor at the next standup.
2. **Your instructor contacts the stakeholder through the school.** You keep building.
3. **If there is still no answer, you contact the backup person** named in your acceptance
   agreement, with your instructor copied.
4. **If nobody from the organization can take part in acceptance,** your instructor designates an
   adult to act for the stakeholder. The acceptance run happens against the **signed criteria,
   unchanged**. The acceptance record states plainly who ran it and why.
5. **The handoff package still goes to the stakeholder in writing**, through the school, even if
   they never reply. Record that it was sent.

Every step goes in your contact log on the day it happens.

## The two failures to catch this phase

**Scope, arriving late.** The earliest signal is a student still building features on Monday of
Week 15 instead of running sessions. Anything that is not a correction from testing waits for the
future improvements list.

**Stakeholder, absent at the moment it matters.** The earliest signal is a Week 15 update with no
reply, with the acceptance run nine days away. Book the acceptance run time in Week 15, in writing.
