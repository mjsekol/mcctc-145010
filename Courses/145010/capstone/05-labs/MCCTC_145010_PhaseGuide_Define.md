# Phase Guide · Define
## 145010 Senior Capstone · Weeks 7-8

**Why this phase exists.** Every capstone that fails, fails here first. It fails because nobody
real wanted it, or because it was too big to finish. Both are visible in Week 8 and both are
nearly impossible to fix in Week 14. Define is two weeks of writing and talking, and it decides
whether the next ten weeks work.

**What you are producing.** A real stakeholder, a concept proposal you present to them, and an
acceptance agreement they sign. **No signature, no build.**

**Competencies this phase evidences:** 2.9.1, 2.9.2, 2.9.3, 2.9.5, 2.9.6 (the project concept
proposal), 1.2.13 (identify stakeholders and solicit their opinions), 1.2.11 (professional
correspondence), 1.10.2 (determine the customer's needs), 1.6.1 (identify business
opportunities), 1.6.5 (organizational structure), 1.6.8 (features and benefits), 1.6.11 (work
within a budget), 1.6.12 (classifications of employee benefits and compensation), 1.9.1 and
1.9.8 (budgets, income sources and expenditures), 1.7.13 (protect intellectual property), 1.3.5
and 1.3.7 (the stakeholder's workplace safety and labor rules), 1.4.7 (productivity tools for the
timeline), 2.12.1 (a written acceptance procedure agreed by the stakeholder and the team).

## What done looks like

At the end of Week 8, all of these are true. Check them yourself before your instructor does.

- [ ] A stakeholder is named in your contact log by role, approved by your instructor, and has
      replied in writing that they are willing to work with you.
- [ ] At least one needs-discovery conversation happened, with a meeting record.
- [ ] `docs/define/stakeholder-finding.md` is committed.
- [ ] `docs/define/concept-proposal.md` is committed, every section filled, and passed the scope
      reality check.
- [ ] The proposal was presented to the stakeholder, and a meeting record says what they asked
      and what changed.
- [ ] `docs/define/acceptance-agreement.md` is committed, and **the signed copy is with your
      instructor**.
- [ ] Your decision log has at least four entries: the problem you chose, the track, the scope
      cut from the scope check, and one more real decision.
- [ ] Your AI usage log exists, even if it has one entry.
- [ ] A commit at the end of every period.
- [ ] AI-Integrated track only: `docs/define/ethics-and-licensing.md` started.

## Week 7

**The goal of the week:** a named, contactable person who has said yes to a conversation.

### Monday · kickoff

- [ ] Hear the kickoff. Read the [Capstone Specification](../09-project/MCCTC_145010_Capstone_Specification.md)
      and the [Capstone Rubric](../09-project/MCCTC_145010_Capstone_Rubric.md).
- [ ] Create your repository with the required structure. Commit it.
- [ ] Open your Problem Inventory. Mark the entries where you can name the person who has the
      problem.
- [ ] Start the [Stakeholder Finding worksheet](MCCTC_145010_Template_StakeholderFinding.md).
      Parts 1 and 2 are due at the end of the build period.
- [ ] Start `decision-log.md`. Entry 1 is the three problems you are considering.

### Tuesday · first contact

- [ ] Finish Part 3 of the worksheet: your top candidate and a backup.
- [ ] **Your instructor approves the candidate before you write to them.**
- [ ] Draft the first contact message using the contact script in the worksheet and the
      [Stakeholder Communication Guide](MCCTC_145010_Guide_StakeholderCommunication.md), section 1.
- [ ] **Your instructor reads the message before it is sent.** Then send it from your school
      account with your instructor copied.
- [ ] Start `docs/communication/contact-log.md`. Every contact, one line each, from today.

### Wednesday and Thursday · the idea conference

Your instructor holds a short conference with every student this week. Come ready to answer these
six questions without notes. If you cannot answer one, that is the thing to work on next.

1. **Who has this problem**, by role, and have you talked to them yet?
2. **What happens today**, step by step, when the problem occurs?
3. **How often does it happen, and how do you know?**
4. **What will they be able to do afterward** that they cannot do now?
5. **What is the smallest thing you could hand them that they would actually use?**
6. **What part do you not yet know how to build**, and when will you find out?

- [ ] Choose your track. Write the decision and the alternative in your decision log.
- [ ] Read your [track guide](../09-project/MCCTC_145010_Capstone_Specification.md#the-three-tracks)
      and [Scope Calibration](../09-project/MCCTC_145010_Capstone_ScopeCalibration.md).
- [ ] Start the concept proposal: sections 1 to 4.
- [ ] If no reply by Thursday, send one polite follow-up and tell your instructor. Do not wait.

### Friday · stakeholder day

- [ ] **Stakeholder named.** Written reply received, instructor approved, recorded in the
      contact log. This is a fixed milestone.
- [ ] Hold the needs-discovery conversation if it can be scheduled today, or schedule it for
      early Week 8. Use the questions in the
      [Meeting Record](MCCTC_145010_Template_MeetingRecord.md#needs-discovery-questions).
- [ ] Write the meeting record the same day.

**No stakeholder by Friday?** Tell your instructor in standup, not at the end of the day. Your
instructor keeps a list of adults who have agreed to be capstone stakeholders and will match you
with one. The project proceeds with that person, and your Problem Inventory entry may change to
fit their problem. That is not a penalty. A project with an imaginary user is.

## Week 8

**The goal of the week:** a proposal that will finish, presented, and signed.

**Plan for a short week.** A school break often lands in Weeks 8-9. That is why the proposal and
agreement go to your stakeholder on **Wednesday**. A signature can come back even if Thursday and
Friday are lost.

### Monday · the full draft

- [ ] [Concept Proposal](MCCTC_145010_Template_ConceptProposal.md): every section drafted, end of
      the build period.
- [ ] Budget section: hours estimated per task, with a total. See the template for how.
- [ ] Stretch goals named.

### Tuesday · the scope reality check

Your instructor reads your proposal and answers one question first: **will you finish this?**

- [ ] Bring the draft, printed or open, and your task breakdown.
- [ ] Write down, word for word, the tasks your instructor says will take four times longer.
- [ ] Make the cuts. Record each one as a decision with the reason.
- [ ] If your instructor says it will not finish, **believe them.** Cut until they say it will.

### Wednesday · send it

- [ ] Final proposal and the [Acceptance Agreement](MCCTC_145010_Template_AcceptanceAgreement.md)
      sent to the stakeholder, with your instructor copied, and a request for a time to present.
- [ ] AI-Integrated track: ethics and licensing analysis, Parts 1 and 2 started.

### Thursday · present it

- [ ] Present the proposal to the stakeholder. In person with your instructor present, on a call
      your instructor approved, or, if neither is possible, in writing with a request for
      questions. **This is 2.9.6.**
- [ ] Ask the stakeholder to read every acceptance criterion aloud or in writing and say whether
      it is what they meant. A criterion two people read differently is a future argument.
- [ ] Meeting record, the same day. What they asked, what changed.

### Friday · stakeholder day

- [ ] **Agreement signed.** The signed copy goes to your instructor. Your repository holds the
      unsigned text and a line in the contact log: "signed agreement version 1.0 received, Week
      8, Friday, on file with instructor."
- [ ] First Friday status note, on the [Weekly Update template](MCCTC_145010_Template_WeeklyStakeholderUpdate.md),
      short form.
- [ ] Commit everything.

**Not signed by Friday?** You do not start building in Week 9. You keep working on Define until it
is signed, and your instructor contacts the stakeholder through the school. See the
[Stakeholder Communication Guide](MCCTC_145010_Guide_StakeholderCommunication.md#8-when-your-stakeholder-goes-quiet).

## The two failures to catch this phase

**No stakeholder.** The earliest signal is a student who, on Tuesday of Week 7, describes the
problem in general terms ("small businesses", "students") instead of naming a person. If that is
you, go back to the worksheet and pick the candidate you can actually reach.

**Oversized scope.** The earliest signal is a proposal whose task breakdown has no hour estimates,
or whose estimates add up to more than fifty build hours. If your total is over fifty, you already
know what the scope check will say.

## Words the WebXam uses for what you did this phase

About a sixth of the WebXam is Strand 2, and Outcome 2.9 is this phase. The exam uses these words.
Know what each one means in your own project.

| Exam word | In your project |
|---|---|
| **Scope** | What your project will and will not do. Your proposal's in-scope and out-of-scope lists. |
| **Purpose** | Why the project exists. Your problem statement. |
| **Branding, scope and purpose of** | How the product looks and sounds, and why it should match the stakeholder's organization. Your proposal's branding section. |
| **Target audience** | Who will use it. Your proposal's audience section. |
| **Client needs** | What the stakeholder must be able to do. Your needs-discovery meeting record. |
| **Expected outcomes, objectives** | The measurable change. Your baseline question and your acceptance criteria. |
| **Deliverables** | The things you hand over. Your proposal's deliverables list. |
| **Communication plan** | How and when you and the stakeholder talk. Your proposal's communication section. |
| **Task breakdown, timeline** | Your task list with hours and weeks. |
| **Costs: equipment, labor** | Your budget section. |
| **Stakeholder** | Anyone affected by or with authority over the project. Not only your main contact. |
| **Proposal** | The document you presented and the stakeholder approved. |
