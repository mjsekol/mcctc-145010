for satisfaction).

---

## Why this sheet looks the way it does

**Two columns, because behavior and opinion are different evidence.** The left column is what they
did. The right column is what they said. When they disagree, the left column wins.

**Times, because "it took a while" is not a finding.** Write the time at the start of every action.
A clock you can see without picking anything up is enough.

**Paper, because nothing is recorded.** Your notes are the only record, which is why you write them
up the same day.

**What to write in the left column:** where they looked first, what they clicked or pressed, every
wrong turn, every pause longer than about five seconds (write "pause" and the length), anything
they read out loud, any time they went back.

**Mark hesitation.** A pause, a hand hovering, a cursor moving back and forth. Write `H` and the
length. Hesitation is often the earliest sign of a problem the person never mentions.


- **Completed** means they reached the result, not that they were satisfied. Write yes, no, or
  "moved on at 3 min".
- **Wrong attempts** means separate tries before the right one, including ones that caused an
  error.
- **Hesitations** means the count of `H` marks for that task.

## Did you break a rule?

Be honest. It is more useful than a clean sheet.

```
[ ] I named a control or said "you can"          On task: ___   What I said: ________________
[ ] I asked "did that work?"                      On task: ___
[ ] I explained or defended the design            On task: ___
[ ] My face gave something away                   On task: ___
[ ] I did not break any of them
```

**A session where you slipped is still usable.** Note which task it happened on, so the finding
from that task carries the caveat.
