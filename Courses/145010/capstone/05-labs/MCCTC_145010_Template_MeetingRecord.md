# Template · Meeting Record and Contact Log
## 145010 Senior Capstone · after every conversation, Weeks 7-18

**Commit as:** one file per conversation in `docs/communication/meetings/`, named
`week-<nn>-<day>-<purpose>.md`. The contact log is `docs/communication/contact-log.md`.
**Due:** the same day as the conversation. A record written two days later is a guess.

**Competencies this evidences:** 1.2.13 (identify stakeholders and solicit their opinions), 1.2.7
(problem-solving and consensus-building to determine next steps), 1.10.2 (determine the customer's
needs), 1.2.12 (technical writing to complete forms and reports), 1.4.7 (productivity tools:
lists, calendars, contacts), 1.6.5 (organizational structure and chain of command).

---

## Why this exists

**Two people leave the same meeting remembering different things.** A record written the same day,
and sent to the stakeholder as a short follow-up, turns a conversation into an agreement. It is also
what your acceptance criteria, your requirements, and half your defense answers are built from.

**The failure to avoid.** Writing what you meant to hear. Write what they said. Put their exact
words in quotation marks when they matter, and mark your own interpretation as yours.

---

## The contact log

One line per contact of any kind: an email sent, a reply received, a meeting, a call, a message
with no reply. **This is the evidence that you did your part** if a stakeholder goes quiet.

```markdown

| Week and day | Direction | Channel | Purpose | Outcome | Record |
```

## Needs-discovery questions

**Use these in your first real conversation, in Week 7 or early Week 8.** You will not ask all of
them. Pick the ones that fit, in roughly this order. Your job is to listen, not to pitch.

**About the problem**
1. Walk me through what happens today, step by step, the last time this came up.
2. How often does it happen? How do you know?
3. What does it cost you when it goes wrong: time, mistakes, money, stress?
4. What have you already tried?
5. If this were fixed, what would you be able to do that you cannot do now?

**About the people**
6. Who else deals with this problem? Who would use a solution?
7. Who would need to approve something new here? *(1.6.5)*
8. Who would look after it once I have graduated?
9. What devices and places would people use it on?

**About the limits**
10. Is there anything I must not touch, see, or store?
11. Are there safety rules or procedures where this would be used? *(1.3.5)*
12. What would make you say this was not worth it?

**About measuring it**
13. Is there a record I could count, with no names in it, that shows how often this happens now?
14. How would you know, a month from now, that it worked?

**Close with:**
> Thank you. I will send you a short summary today so you can correct anything I got wrong.

**What not to do:**
- **Do not describe your solution.** The moment you do, they start reacting to it instead of
  telling you about the problem.
- **Do not fill silences.** People often say the most useful thing after a pause.
- **Do not ask "would you use an app that...?"** Almost everyone says yes to be polite, and it
  tells you nothing.
