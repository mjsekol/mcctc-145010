## Why this exists

**This is the page you will send to an employer, a college, or an internship interviewer.** Two
years of work end up in a sentence on a résumé and a story in an interview. Writing both now, while
you remember every detail, is worth more than any polish later.

**The failure to avoid is describing the technology instead of the result.** "Built a Flask app
with a SQLite database" describes a thousand student projects. "Built a shift sign-up tool a youth
league coordinator used for five weeks, and empty shifts went from about one and a half a week to
<n>" describes yours.

**The second failure is sharing what is not yours to share.** Your stakeholder's name, their
customers, their documents, their screens with real data. Ask before you publish anything about
them, and when in doubt, describe them by role.

## The résumé line

One line. A strong verb, what you built, for whom, and a result.

```
Built and handed over a <what> for a <stakeholder role at an organization type>, <result>;
passed <n> of <n> acceptance criteria and ran <n> days without intervention.
```

## The interview story

When an interviewer says "tell me about a project," you have about two minutes. Write the story
in four parts, and practice it out loud.

```
The situation:  <who had what problem>
What I had to do: <what I agreed to deliver, and the hard part>
What I did:     <two or three decisions, including one that went wrong and how I fixed it>
The result:     <what changed, measured, and what the stakeholder said>
```
