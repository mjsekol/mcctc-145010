## Why this exists

**The panel has ten minutes to understand twelve weeks.** They do not need every feature. They
need to know what problem you solved, for whom, how you know it worked, and whether you understand
what you built. A tour of screens answers none of those.

**Plan on about ten minutes presenting and five minutes of questions.** Your instructor confirms
the lengths and who is on the panel. The audience may be your instructor, the advisory committee,
an employer panel, or underclassmen. Ask which, because it changes how much you explain.

**The failure to avoid is a demo with no plan for failure.** Live demos break on stage. It happens
to professionals. What the panel scores is what you do next.

**When it breaks on stage:**
1. **Say what broke, in one sentence.** "The hosted page is not answering."
2. **Say what you will do.** "I will show the same steps on my local copy."
3. **Do it.** Do not debug in front of the panel for more than thirty seconds.
4. **Come back to it once, briefly, at the end**, if there is time.

**What not to do:** apologize repeatedly, blame the network at length, or pretend it did not happen.
