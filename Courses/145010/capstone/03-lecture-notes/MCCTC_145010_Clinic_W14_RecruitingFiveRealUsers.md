**The signal:** fewer than five participants with confirmed times, or a recruiting table that lists

**Slides:** This clinic has no slide outline. It runs from the board.

**If you missed it,** you can learn the skill from this file alone.

**Competencies:** 6.5.10 (develop and execute usability tests on a completed website), 2.12.4

---

## The idea in plain language

**Five people, outside this class, who have never seen your project, each booked for a day and a
time in Week 15, with a backup.** You find them this week, because a student who starts on Monday of
Week 15 does not find five.

## Why it exists

A usability session measures what a stranger does with your project. A classmate is not a stranger.
They know what a sign-up page usually looks like in this room, they know you, and they want you to
do well. Their session tells you what a classmate does.

**Recruiting is the part that takes longest and feels least like programming.** Adults are busy. A
message sent today gets an answer Friday, maybe. That is why this clinic is in Week 14.

### The rules that apply the moment you ask someone

These come from the [Usability Test Protocol](../05-labs/MCCTC_145010_Template_UsabilityTestProtocol.md).
They are conditions, not advice.

- **No classmates in the formal five.** A classmate is for Monday's pilot.
- **Participants are P1 to P5.** No names, ages, or job titles in anything you write.
- **No recording of any kind.** Say so when you ask.
- **Never alone with an adult you do not know.** At school, on an approved call, or on a visit the
  school arranged.
- **Every message goes through school channels**, with your instructor copied.

---

## Who to ask, best first

1. **The real users**, reached through your stakeholder and your instructor.
2. **Adults in this building outside the program.** Your instructor keeps a list of people who have
   agreed.
3. **Students in other programs** who have never seen your project.
4. **Family at home**, with every rule above. A younger sibling only if they are twelve or older and
   a parent is in the room.

---

## Worked example 1 · asking the stakeholder for real users

The garden's volunteers are the real users. The student cannot contact them directly, so the ask
goes to the coordinator, from school email, instructor copied.

```
Subject: Garden sign-up: could two or three volunteers try it next week?

Hello <title and last name>,

Next week I am testing the sign-up page with people who have never used it.
Each session takes about fifteen minutes. I watch them try a few tasks and
take notes on paper. Nothing is recorded and I do not write down names.

Could you ask two or three volunteers whether they would help? The sessions
can happen at school on Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday, or during the
volunteer meeting you mentioned, if my instructor can arrange a visit.

If they agree, could you reply with the days that suit them? I will not
contact them directly.

Thank you,
<first name>
```

**The result it gets:** the coordinator knows exactly what is being asked, how long it takes, and
that the volunteers' privacy is protected. The student never holds a volunteer's contact details.

## Worked example 2 · the recruiting table, filled in

```
## Recruiting
| P      | Kind of participant     | Why they fit                           | Session: Week 15   | Where                   |
| P1     | real user               | garden volunteer, signs up weekly      | Tue, 12:40         | school, library room    |
| P2     | real user               | new volunteer, has not used the page   | Wed, 12:40         | school, library room    |
| P3     | building adult          | from the instructor's list, not tech   | Wed, Period 8      | school, conference room |
| P4     | other program           | culinary student, never seen it        | Thu, 12:40         | school, lab corner      |
| P5     | family                  | grandparent, uses a phone for email    | Thu evening        | home, parent present    |
| Backup | building adult          | from the instructor's list             | Thu, Period 8      | school                  |

Who these five do not represent: nobody who uses a screen reader; nobody whose
first language is not English; nobody younger than their late teens.
```

**Read the last line.** It is honest, and it is your 1.5.5 evidence. It also tells you what to try to
fix: the backup could be someone who covers one of those gaps.

## Worked example 3 · what you say in person

The script from the protocol, word for word:

```
I am testing a project I built for a real client, and I need to watch a few
people try it. It takes about fifteen minutes. I am testing the project, not
you, and nothing is recorded. Would you be willing to help, and is there a
time this week that works?
```

**When someone says "maybe":** thank them and ask for a specific time. "Maybe" without a time is a
no you have not heard yet. Keep asking until the table has five times and a backup.

## The wrong version, and what it produces

```
## Recruiting
| P  | Kind of participant | Session        |
| P1 | Jalen (classmate)   | whenever       |
| P2 | Marcus (classmate)  | whenever       |
| P3 | my mom              | TBD            |
| P4 | ask someone Monday  |                |
| P5 | ask someone Monday  |                |
```

**What it produces:** two sessions that break the classmate rule and do not count, real first names
in a committed file, two empty rows, and no times. On Tuesday of Week 15 this student has one
session, maybe. By Friday the findings table says "1 of 1," which describes nothing. The usability
evidence in the rubric is scored from counts out of real users.

## Why the wrong version is tempting

Classmates are right there, they say yes, and they do not need an email. Asking an adult you do not
know well feels awkward, and waiting for replies feels like wasted time. **The awkward message this
week is what makes next week possible.** Every student in the room is asking at the same time, so
the instructor's list of adults fills up fast.

---

## What to do in your project today

1. Send the stakeholder message in example 1, adapted to your project, through school email with your
   instructor copied. Log it in your contact log.
2. Ask your instructor for two names from the list of adults who have agreed.
3. Start the recruiting table in `docs/control/usability/protocol.md`. P-numbers only.
4. Write "Who these five do not represent" now, and try to recruit your backup to cover a gap.
5. Put the session times in your Friday [Weekly Stakeholder Update](../05-labs/MCCTC_145010_Template_WeeklyStakeholderUpdate.md).
6. Pick a classmate for Monday's pilot. Label it as a pilot.
7. Print five copies of the [Observation Sheet](../05-labs/MCCTC_145010_Template_UsabilityObservationSheet.md)
   before Monday.

## Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Participant** | A person who tries your project in a session, recorded only as P1 to P5 |
| **Pilot** | A practice session with a classmate, to fix the tasks before real sessions |
| **Recruiting** | Finding and booking participants, with a time and a place |
| **Backup** | A sixth booked person, in case one of the five cancels |
| **Representation** | Which kinds of people your five include, and which they leave out |
