# Gate 2 · Week 8 · The Proposal That Could Not Finish
## 145010 Senior Capstone · Week 8, Monday · first 40 minutes of the build period · Define

**40 minutes. Individual. Silent. No AI tool.** Read the folder on screen or on paper. Write your
answers on paper, or in a plain editor with every AI feature turned off.

**Rubric:** AI Output Evaluation (Part A), plus a short scope reality check practice (Part B).

**Slot:** Week 8, Monday, the first 40 minutes of the build period, **before you finish your own
proposal draft.** The reveal is the first 10 minutes of Tuesday's build period, before the scope
reality check conferences.

**Competencies:** 2.9.2 (scope and purpose of the project), 2.9.3 (target audience, client needs,
budget), 2.9.5 (task breakdown, costs, labor), 1.9.1 (create and interpret a budget), 1.10.2
(determine the customer's needs), 1.2.1 (extract valid information and cite its source).

---

## Why this exists

**Tomorrow your instructor reads your proposal and answers one question first: will you finish
this?** Most drafts that get a "no" were not careless. They were fluent, organized, and confident,
which is exactly what an AI draft is.

**A proposal is a promise.** Everything after Week 8 is measured against it. A wrong total, a need
the stakeholder never stated, or a number nobody can trace all become things you promised in writing.
Today you find them in someone else's draft, so tonight you can find them in yours.

---

## The situation

A senior in a composite version of this program found a stakeholder: the shop coordinator of a
volunteer-run community bike repair co-op. Volunteers fix donated bikes on open-shop nights. When a
parts bin is empty, the repair stalls, and nobody tells the coordinator.

On Week 7, Friday, the senior held the needs-discovery conversation and wrote the meeting record.
That evening the senior gave the record to the lab's local assistant and asked it to draft seven
sections of the concept proposal. **The senior was about to paste the draft in as the Monday
proposal.**

## Part A · Score the draft

**Score it on the five AI Output Evaluation parameters.**

| Parameter | The question it asks here |
|---|---|
| **Validity** | Is what it says true? Do its numbers add up? |
| **Relevance** | Is it about the problem the coordinator named? |
| **Authenticity** | Does it quote or attribute anything the coordinator did not say? |
| **Potential Bias** | Does it assume something about a group of people that the record contradicts, and who does that leave out? |
| **Hallucinations** | Does it state something as fact that exists nowhere, in the folder or anywhere else? |

**There is at least one real problem under every one of the five.** There is also **one item that
is genuinely arguable.** It is not one of the five.

**There is also one planned task that breaks a program rule.** Finding it scores nothing. Name it
at the top of your sheet anyway, because in a real proposal it stops the work.

### What to hand in for Part A

For each of the five findings, all four of these:

```
Parameter        which of the five
The sentence     quoted, with its location (3.2, N2, T11, 15.1)
The evidence     the record line (L), the prompt line (Q), or your arithmetic
What it costs    what goes wrong for the senior or the coordinator if this is signed
```

Plus one entry for the sentence you are unsure about:

```
The sentence              quoted, with its location
The case that it is fine
The case that it is not
What would settle it      the one thing you would check or ask
```

**You are not scored on which side you land on.** You are scored on whether both cases are real.

## Part B · The scope reality check, practice

**Read the draft the way your instructor will read yours tonight.** Answer in this order. Order
matters: the answer to question 1 comes first, before any explanation.

1. **Will this finish? Yes or no, first.** Then one sentence with the number that decides it.
2. **The three tasks that will take four times longer than the draft thinks.** Give each task's ID
   and one reason.
3. **The minimum viable version.** Three to five sentences. Keep what the coordinator said matters,
   and name what moves to the stretch list.

## Two things worth saying before you start

**The draft is not all wrong.** Section 1 is a fair summary of the record, and several needs,
features, and budget rows are correct. A sheet that marks every section has not read the record.

**One of the problems is arithmetic.** It takes about a minute with the numbers in section 11,
and it is the one most people walk past, because the draft tells you the total is fine.

| Part B, question 2 | 1 |
| Part B, question 3 | 1 |
| **Total** | **9** |

**Why the hallucination carries the double penalty.** An invented fact in a proposal goes into a
signed document, then into your Week 18 presentation, then into a panelist's notes. Every step
makes it look more checked, and none of them checked it.

## One rule for today

**You may not write anything on your sheet that you cannot point at in the folder**, or show with
arithmetic. That is the same rule the draft broke.

**Then finish your own draft.** Before you commit it, add up your own section 11, and put a source
next to every number in your section 15.
