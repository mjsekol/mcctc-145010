---

## Why you are doing this today

**You write your own test plan in this build period.** Before you do, you read one that looks
finished and is not. A test plan is the document most likely to be generated by an AI and least
likely to be checked, because it reads like a checklist and checklists feel safe.

**A test plan that cannot fail protects nobody.** It passes in Week 10, it passes in Week 16, and the
stakeholder finds the problems in Week 17. The habits you use on this plan are the ones you use on
yours an hour from now.

---

## The situation

A senior is building the Parts Bin Board for the shop coordinator of a volunteer bike repair co-op.
Volunteers mark a parts bin low. The coordinator sees the low list and marks bins restocked.

On Wednesday of Week 10 the senior pasted the requirements and the architecture into an AI assistant
and asked for the written test plan, using the course template. The plan came back tidy, complete
looking, and on the right template. The senior skimmed it and was about to commit it.

**Your job is to read it before they commit it.**

## Your job

**Score it on the five AI Output Evaluation parameters.**

```
Validity        is what it says true, given the evidence
Relevance       is it about the thing that was asked
Authenticity    is it what it claims to be, from where it claims
Potential Bias  does it treat people or evidence unevenly
Hallucinations  does it contain things that are not there
```

**There is at least one real problem under every one of the five.** There is also **one item that is
genuinely arguable**, where a reasonable person could go either way, and it is not one of the five.

## What to submit

### Five findings, one per parameter

For each, all four of these:

```
Parameter        which of the five
The sentence     quoted, with its location (section, paragraph number, or case ID)
The evidence     the file and line that contradicts it, or the command and what it printed
What it costs    what the senior, the coordinator, or a volunteer would get wrong because of it
```

**"What it costs" separates a full finding from a half one.** A finding with no consequence is an
observation.

### Plus one entry: the thing you are unsure about

```
The sentence            quoted, with its location
The case that it is fine
The case that it is not
What would settle it     the one thing you would check or change
```

**You are not scored on which side you land on.** You are scored on whether both cases are real.

## Before you start

**The plan is not all wrong.** Several cases are good ones, with a starting state and an expected
result that could fail. A student who marks every line as a defect has not read it.

**One problem is about time.** Check what the plan claims happened against what the architecture
says existed on that day.

**One problem is about who is missing.** Read NF4 and then read section 2 slowly.

## Scoring

| Item | Points |
|---|---|
| Validity finding | 1 |
| Relevance finding | 1 |
| Authenticity finding | 1 |
| Potential Bias finding | 1 |
| **Hallucination finding** | **1, and missing it costs 2** |
| The unsure-about entry, with both cases | 1 |
| **Total** | **6, and the lowest possible score is negative** |

**Why the hallucination carries the double penalty.** Something invented in a test plan does not
stay in the test plan. It gets relied on during the day that matters, and it reaches a stakeholder
as if it were proof. That is this course's security defect.
