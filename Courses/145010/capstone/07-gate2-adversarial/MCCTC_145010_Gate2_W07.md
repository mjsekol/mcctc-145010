# Gate 2 · Week 7 · The Message That Promised Too Much
## 145010 Senior Capstone · Week 7, Tuesday · first 40 minutes of the build period · Define

**40 minutes. Individual. Silent. No AI tool.** Read the folder on screen or on paper. Write your
answers on paper, or in a plain editor with every AI feature turned off.

**Rubric:** AI Output Evaluation. Validity, Relevance, Authenticity, Potential Bias,
Hallucinations.

**Slot:** Week 7, Tuesday, the first 40 minutes of the build period, **before you draft your own
first contact message.** The reveal is the first 10 minutes of Wednesday's build period.

**Competencies:** 1.2.11 (professional correspondence), 1.2.13 (identify stakeholders and solicit
their opinions), 1.4.8 (electronic communication and etiquette), 1.1.4 (networking to build
professional relationships), 1.2.1 (extract relevant, valid information and cite its source).

---

## Why this exists

**Today you write to a stranger adult for the first time.** You will be tempted to ask an AI to
write it. The draft will come back fluent, warm, and confident. It will also say things you never
said, promise things you cannot keep, and describe a person you have never met.

**A first message is hard to take back.** The person reads it once and decides whether you are
worth fifteen minutes. A made-up memory or a promise you cannot keep ends the relationship before
it starts, and your instructor is copied on it.

So before you write your own, you take one apart.

---

## The situation

A senior in a composite version of this program chose the career center's media specialist as a
stakeholder candidate. The media center lends cameras, tripods, and laptops to staff, and the
checkout runs from a paper binder. The instructor approved the candidate.

The senior wrote notes, pasted them into the lab's local assistant, and asked for a first email.
**The senior was about to send the draft unchanged.** The instructor asked to read it first.

**You are the instructor's second reader.**

## Your job

**Score the email on the five AI Output Evaluation parameters.**

| Parameter | The question it asks here |
|---|---|
| **Validity** | Is what it says true, given the notes? |
| **Relevance** | Is it the email the senior asked for? |
| **Authenticity** | Is it what it claims to be? Does it claim a history or a quotation that is not real? |
| **Potential Bias** | Does it treat the reader unfairly, based on something assumed rather than known? |
| **Hallucinations** | Does it describe something that does not exist? |

**There is at least one real problem under every one of the five.** There is also **one item that
is genuinely arguable**, where a reasonable person could go either way. It is not one of the five.

**There is also one sentence that breaks a program rule.** Finding it scores nothing. Missing it
is worse than a lost point, because in a real draft it would stop the message from going out. If
you see it, name it at the top of your sheet.

---

## What to hand in

### Five findings, one per parameter

For each finding, all four of these:

```
Parameter        which of the five
The sentence     quoted, with its location (S, or P1 to P9)
The evidence     the line in student-notes.md that settles it (L1 to L19)
What it costs    what goes wrong for the senior or the specialist if it is sent
```

**"What it costs" is what separates a strong finding from an observation.** "P5 is wrong" is an
observation. "P5 tells the specialist the project takes about an hour, so the first weekly check-in
feels like a broken promise" is a finding.

### Plus one entry: the sentence you are unsure about

```
The sentence              quoted, with its location
The case that it is fine
The case that it is not
What would settle it      the one thing you would check or ask
```

**You are not scored on which side you land on.** You are scored on whether both cases are real.

## Two things worth saying before you start

**The email is not all wrong.** Several sentences in it are correct and supported by the notes. A
sheet that marks every paragraph as a defect has not read the notes either.

**Two of the problems are about something missing.** The notes do not contain it, and the email
states it as fact. Those are the ones most people walk past, because you are reading for what is
there.

## Scoring

| Item | Points |
|---|---|
| Validity finding | 1 |
| Relevance finding | 1 |
| Authenticity finding | 1 |
| Potential Bias finding | 1 |
| **Hallucination finding** | **1, and missing it costs 2** |
| The unsure-about entry, with both cases | 1 |
| **Total** | **6, and the lowest possible score is negative** |

**Why the hallucination carries the double penalty.** A made-up program in a first message is the
sentence the specialist repeats to someone else. When they find out it does not exist, they stop
trusting everything else you wrote, and the person who looks foolish is them.

## One rule for today

**You may not write anything on your sheet that you cannot point at in `student-notes.md`.** That
is the same rule the draft broke.

**Then write your own message.** The rest of today's build period is your first contact draft.
